Built from the field

DestrierOS grew out of nine years in oil field operations, where critical work was scattered across spreadsheets, inboxes, paperwork apps, telematics portals, and phone reminders. Every handoff meant another chance to lose the context needed to finish the job.

After years of looking for a connected system, Schardt Industries founder Matthew Schardt went to Carnegie Mellon University to develop the technical skills to build it. The first lines of DestrierOS were written on an oil rig. The product remains self-funded while its founder continues working in the field, keeping development close to the conditions crews and operators face every day.

Follow the work from first call to closeout

A customer request should carry its history into dispatch, field execution, review, and billing. DestrierOS keeps those records connected so each team can see what happened, what is missing, and who owns the next step.

  1. Start with the customer. Connect leads, contacts, quotes, messages, service requests, and account history.
  2. Plan and dispatch. Bring together work orders, crew schedules, vehicle locations, asset readiness, and required parts.
  3. Capture the work. Collect time, forms, photos, materials, documents, and signoff evidence from the field and shared terminals.
  4. Review and close. Check required evidence, approvals, invoice handoffs, job costs, and operational reporting.

Designed for real operating conditions

DestrierOS is built for industrial service businesses, fleet-heavy operations, distributed crews, and teams with demanding documentation and compliance needs. That means planning for changing schedules, missing parts, weak signal, shared terminals, and evidence that has to make it back to the office.

Mobile development focuses on retaining field work through connection loss and making sync conflicts visible. Sensitive actions require an online check when current permissions or provider confirmation are needed. Kiosk events and fleet signals return to the same company records used by dispatch, HR, and management.

Access, evidence & engineering discipline

Company-scoped records, role and workspace permissions, record-level controls, and audit history form the platform's access model. Documents, HR information, customer records, and fleet data stay connected to the people authorized to use them.

Development includes regression testing, recurring security and performance audits, accessibility checks, recovery planning, and release reviews. Mobile acceptance includes physical-device validation of workflows, permissions, offline behavior, and location policies.
